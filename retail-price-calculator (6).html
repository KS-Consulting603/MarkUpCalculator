Foreign cost is entered per unit, not as a line total — along with currency, qty, and weight, that's the entire set of entered figures per row. Everything else is calculated, starting with the line's total foreign cost (cost/unit × qty) and including the five right-most columns, which take that chain all the way back down to a per-unit basis — the same numbers, just spread across however many units they actually cover. Leave qty blank or at 0 and every calculated column, not just the per-unit ones, shows – rather than a wrong number, since the line total itself depends on a real quantity now. The rate for each currency is fetched live from Yahoo Finance through corsproxy.io (free for a github.io URL, no key) — that's the spot rate shown above. Each row's calculation uses that rate reduced by the buffer % (shown in the row itself, not above) — e.g. a 5% buffer on a spot rate of 0.88 uses 0.836, protecting against the exchange rate moving against you between quoting and paying. Tariff and shipping are both folded into a total cost before margin is applied, so the margin % is genuinely achieved on the full landed cost, not diluted by real costs added afterward at 0% margin (an earlier version of this tool did exactly that with tariff, and a real handling charge was found missing from the calculation entirely before that). Tariff is a single entry above, applied to every row — this assumes one order means one country of origin, which is worth revisiting if that ever stops being true. Shipping is different from every other cost here: for drop-shipped orders the real charge often isn't known until well after the quote goes out, so it's estimated instead of entered — each row's own weight (in pounds) × the $/lb rate above × (1 + the shipping buffer), computed directly per row rather than allocated from one shared total. The $/lb rate started from a researched public baseline for FedEx International Priority-style Europe-to-US rates; it's meant to be tuned as real shipments provide better data, not treated as precise. Tariff rates aren't published as market data the way exchange rates are, so that field stays a manual entry — click into it, or into a row's currency, to populate the reference panel above with the current US import tariff link for that currency. Nothing auto-refreshes; rates only change when you click Update Rates. Export CSV stamps the rates, margin, tariff, and shipping rate/buffer used, for the record.
